# ATLAS — MASTER PROJECT CONTEXT & PRODUCT VISION
## Building a Personal Operating System and Human Development Platform

You are joining an existing software project called **ATLAS**.

Your role is to act as the senior software architect, product engineer, UI/UX designer, and implementation partner.

This document establishes the product vision, philosophy, requirements, design direction, and long-term development strategy of ATLAS.

Read and understand the entire document before making modifications.

---

# PART 1 — WHAT IS ATLAS?

ATLAS is my personal operating system.

It is designed to help me organise, understand, manage, and improve different aspects of my everyday life.

However, I want to evolve it considerably beyond a conventional productivity application.

Most productivity applications focus on:
- Tasks
- Calendars
- Habits
- Reminders
- Notes
- Time management
- Daily productivity

These are useful, but they only represent a small fraction of human life.

I want ATLAS to eventually become a comprehensive personal intelligence system that understands the person using it.

It should help answer questions such as:

- Who am I today?
- Who do I want to become?
- What characteristics define the person I want to be?
- How healthy am I?
- How intelligent and knowledgeable am I becoming?
- Am I developing meaningful relationships?
- Am I building financial independence?
- Am I progressing professionally?
- Am I experiencing life or simply completing tasks?
- Which areas of my life am I neglecting?
- What should I improve next?
- How have I evolved over the last year, five years, or decade?

The fundamental philosophy:

**ATLAS should track the evolution of a human being, not merely the completion of tasks.**

I want to build something that combines:
1. A personal operating system.
2. A human development framework.
3. A life-tracking and reflection platform.
4. A long-term personal analytics system.
5. An intelligent assistant that helps me understand and improve my life.

Think of the character-development mechanics of BitLife, combined with a sophisticated personal operating system, but designed for deliberate real-world development rather than gamification alone.

The user is the character.
Life is the environment.
Activities are the actions.
Attributes represent development.
Milestones represent achievements.
Time represents progression.

ATLAS previously used a dark black-and-green DOOMSDAY visual identity.

I want to retain the entire existing visual direction but replace its green colour palette with purple.

This is a colour-system transformation, NOT a complete redesign.

The existing DOOMSDAY design language must remain intact.

Preserve:

- Near-black backgrounds.
- Layered dark surfaces.
- Futuristic visual language.
- Premium interface aesthetics.
- Existing layouts and component hierarchy.
- Typography.
- Spacing.
- Component shapes.
- Existing visual depth.
- Motion and animation behaviour.

3. Replace green active navigation indicators with purple.
4. Replace green focus rings and selection indicators with purple.
5. Replace green chart accents with purple where they represent the old brand accent.

Do not blindly replace every green colour in the codebase.

For example, green may represent success or positive financial performance. Semantic colours should retain their meaning where appropriate.

Use centralised design tokens wherever possible so the colour identity can be changed consistently in the future.

Do not redesign existing screens just because the palette is changing.

The intended result should look like the exact same ATLAS interface, but with a sophisticated purple identity instead of green.

Think:

BLACK + PURPLE + CINEMATIC DEPTH.

Not:
- Bright purple consumer SaaS.
- Pastel purple interfaces.
- Generic neon cyberpunk.
- Excessive glowing effects.
- Entirely purple backgrounds.

Purple should function as the signal/accent colour against the dark interface.

# PART 6 — CURRENT SELF VS IDEAL SELF

This is the most important feature.

Every user should have two conceptual profiles.

## Current Self

A representation of who the person is today.

Includes:
- Current attributes
- Current measurements
- Current financial position
- Current health measurements
- Current relationships
- Current skills
- Current achievements
- Current habits
- Current lifestyle
- Current personality assessments

## Ideal Self

A representation of who the person wants to become.

Users should define:
- Desired physical condition
- Desired financial position
- Desired career
- Desired knowledge
- Desired relationships
- Desired experiences
- Desired lifestyle
- Desired personality traits
- Desired accomplishments
- Desired legacy

For each relevant attribute, store:
1. Current state.
2. Desired state.
3. Why the user wants it.
4. Definition of success.
5. Supporting activities.
6. Historical progress.
7. Relevant milestones.

Example:

PHYSICAL FITNESS

Current:
70/100

Target:
90/100

Vision:
Develop an athletic, strong, healthy body with excellent endurance and mobility.

Supporting activities:
- Gym
- Cardio
- Nutrition
- Recovery
- Sleep

Not every attribute needs a numerical target. Some goals should be narrative or milestone-based.

Ideal Self must remain editable as the user's priorities evolve.

# PART 12 — LIFE INTELLIGENCE

In the future, ATLAS should analyse development and identify patterns.

Examples:

"You have exercised consistently for three months, but your endurance measurements have remained unchanged."

"You have made progress professionally but have allocated little time to creative activities."

"Your savings rate has improved over the past six months."

"Your current goals are heavily concentrated in career and financial development."

Capabilities:
- Pattern identification
- Stagnation detection
- Historical comparison
- Development insights
- Suggested actions
- Periodic reflections
- Goal prioritisation

AI should interpret information. Deterministic calculations should remain in application logic.

Do not invent measurements, imply unsupported causality, or provide medical or psychological diagnoses.

Insights must be traceable to underlying data.

# PART 13 — PRODUCT PHILOSOPHY

ATLAS is not a toxic productivity application.

Rest, happiness, relationships, exploration, and doing nothing are valid parts of life.

Principles:

1. Human worth is not determined by metrics.
2. Not every dimension needs a numerical score.
3. The user defines their own ideal self.
4. Goals can evolve.
5. Progress is not always linear.
6. Rest and enjoyment are legitimate.
7. Financial success does not automatically equal life success.
8. Overall scores must not conceal imbalances.
9. Personal data should remain private and under user control.
10. The system should encourage reflection, not anxiety.
